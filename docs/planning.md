# Planning

Built: Zitadel login; import from text, photo, website and Instagram posts; Postgres storage with images; recipe lineage (save overwrites, a progression is a new version that takes the line's pin, a variant starts a line of its own, and deleting a version takes its progressions with it); collections, tags, filters, preferences, scaling, US/metric display, printing and similar-recipe search. Work is scheduled in Waves by the rules in [repo_rules.md](repo_rules.md); open work lives in the GitHub issues and milestones.

Current direction: a central ingredient store (#170). It is seeded from Open Food Facts entries that have an FDC id, and after a recipe is saved each ingredient line is fuzzy matched against it; cooks who opt in confirm close matches, which adds aliases and new entries. Matching runs off the request path (#172): a save queues the recipe in Postgres, and workers claim it with `SKIP LOCKED`. The opt-in is the preference `ingredientMatching: "on"` (#173). Opting in queues the cook's saved recipes again, all at once or a batch at a time as they choose in the opt-in dialog (#180). A name two entries share across the languages searched is asked rather than linked, until entries can be merged (#182); a recipe of unknown language (`und`) creates no entries until its language is looked up (#183). Recipes read each line's entry, joined rather than stored, and ingredient filters match an entry's names in any language (#181). Only units native to one system convert: in metric, cups and fluid ounces of a dry entry with a density show as grams, while spoons and counts are the same in every system and stay as written. An entry is liquid by the cooks' first answer, or until then by a density of 0.95–1.1 g/ml. Shopping lists (#84), the pantry, translation and substitutions build on its ids. Known gaps: a photo of two pages comes back as one blended recipe; the app can't tell how well the model read a page; and every text and photo import sends the user's recipe to Google.
